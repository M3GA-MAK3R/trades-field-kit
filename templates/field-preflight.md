# Field Pre-Flight & Toolbox Talk Aid

> A discussion aid that reinforces discipline, not a substitute for it.
> Use this aid to discuss scope, hazards, responsibilities, tools, and handoff
> before work begins, and revisit it when conditions change. It does not replace
> required paperwork, employer safety programs, equipment-specific procedures,
> training, supervision, or authorization. Checking a box or completing a prompt
> does not establish that work is safe, compliant, or complete. Each person remains
> responsible for following procedures, communicating concerns, and stopping when
> conditions are unsafe or unclear. This aid cannot compensate for poor preparation,
> lack of discipline, or lack of work ethic.

## Use and Record Boundaries

- **Public repository:** Blank aids and sanitized examples only. Do not submit
  completed job records, customer contacts, facility details, or job photos through
  commits, issues, pull requests, or other public channels.
- **Actual job records:** Complete and retain required paperwork in the
  employer-approved system of record. Discuss sensitive details through approved
  private channels, not in this public repository.
- **Portfolio examples:** Use only material you have permission to share, with
  identifying and sensitive information removed.
- **How to use:** Ask the prompts aloud, invite questions, and confirm understanding.
  An unanswered question calls for clarification, not a guessed answer or automatic
  checkmark. Record decisions and required approvals in the official paperwork.

## Scope and Responsibilities

- **Work scope:** What are we doing today? What is explicitly outside the scope?
- **Expected outcome:** What would acceptable completion look like according to
  the work order and applicable instructions?
- **Roles:** Who leads the task, who is authorized for each activity, and who is
  assisting or learning under supervision?
- **Points of contact:** Who coordinates site access, shutdowns, BAS operation,
  customer communication, and escalation? Where are their approved contact details?
- **Required records:** Which work order, permits, risk assessment, and other
  employer-required documents apply, and where are they maintained?

## Arrival, Contact, and Waiting

- **Before dispatch:** What arrival/check-in window, approved entrance, parking
  location, escort, access restrictions, and contact method have been agreed?
  Who is the primary site contact, backup contact, and dispatch/supervisor escalation
  contact? Keep their details in the approved private system.
- **Arrived early:** Have we distinguished arrival time from the agreed contact
  and work-start window? Use the agreed early-arrival check-in method, if any;
  do not assume the contact is available early or that arrival authorizes access.
- **Initial check-in:** Through the approved channel, communicate who we are,
  the work-order reference, that we have arrived, and a callback request.
  Share only necessary information and do not disclose access codes or sensitive
  site details in unsecured messages.
- **No answer:** What contact attempts, follow-up interval, and escalation order
  have the shop and site agreed? Use approved backup contacts and dispatch/supervisor
  channels, not guessed contacts or repeated calls on an invented schedule.
  If no plan exists, ask dispatch/supervision for direction.
- **Where to wait:** Is there an approved, safe waiting location that keeps
  entrances, exits, traffic, and emergency access clear? If not, remain at a safe,
  lawful off-site location and tell dispatch where you can be reached.
- **Waiting boundary:** No response is not permission to enter, start work,
  access equipment, bypass security, or follow someone through a controlled entrance.
  Follow the shop/site instructions; do not begin work merely to appear productive.
  Review permitted manuals or the work scope only if safe and allowed; do not
  unload tools, stage equipment, or take site photos without the applicable permission.
- **Contact reached:** Confirm the contact's role and authority. Read back the
  equipment/task, approved scope, access/escort arrangements, affected operations,
  restrictions, and required permits or approvals. Ask for clarification and
  confirm mutual understanding before proceeding. Site access permission does
  not replace task authorization or the crew's safety responsibilities.
- **Still waiting or conditions change:** Who decides whether to continue waiting,
  leave, redirect, or reschedule, and when is the next agreed check-in?
  Escalate unsafe waiting conditions or a changed scope; do not make an
  unauthorized start or silently abandon the assignment. For an actual emergency,
  follow the applicable site/emergency plan, not the routine no-answer sequence.
- **Private record and handoff:** In the official system, record arrival time,
  contact attempts, responses, instructions, waiting status, next check-in/owner,
  and departure or revised appointment as applicable. Keep the record factual,
  not accusatory; this aid is not a timesheet or billing authorization.

## Hazards and Approved Controls

- **Hazard discussion:** What could hurt us or someone nearby? Consider electrical
  and stored energy, pressure, moving equipment, heat, chemicals, access, weather,
  and other site-specific conditions.
- **Applicable procedures:** Which equipment-specific energy-control procedure and
  other approved instructions apply? Who is authorized to perform and verify them?
- **Isolation and verification:** Where will isolation points, applicable energy
  sources, stored-energy controls, and verification be documented under that procedure?
  A meter reading alone is not a blanket verification of all hazardous energy.
- **People affected:** Who must be notified or coordinated with before the work?
- **PPE and readiness:** What protection, qualifications, permits, and site-required
  preparations are needed? Is anyone unclear about the plan?

This aid is not a lockout/tagout procedure or authorization to perform energized
testing. Refer to the employer's applicable program and equipment-specific
instructions; [OSHA's hazardous-energy standard](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147)
is a supporting reference, not a replacement for those instructions.

## Tools, Staging, and Evidence

- **Tools and materials:** Are the correct tools, suitable instruments, instructions,
  and materials available and ready for the approved task?
- **Staging:** Where can equipment be staged without blocking access, exits, or
  other workers? How will tools and loose parts be accounted for?
- **Photos:** Are photos permitted? Which as-found, nameplate, work-in-progress,
  and as-left images are useful, and where must they be stored privately?
  Do not create an exposure or interrupt safe work just to obtain a photograph.
- **Measurements:** What readings are actually needed, by whom, and during which
  authorized work phase? Do not take unnecessary readings to fill a template.
- **Context:** In the approved service record, include equipment identity,
  refrigerant where relevant, operating mode, measurement location, units, time,
  conditions, specification source, and before/after values as applicable.

## Stop-Work and Change Discussion

- **Pause triggers:** What would make us stop: unexpected energy, unsafe access,
  missing instructions, unsuitable tools, unclear scope, or another hazard?
- **Response:** Who do we notify, and how do we leave the area and equipment in a
  safe condition under the applicable procedure?
- **Changed conditions:** If the scope, crew, equipment condition, or hazards change,
  what must be reassessed and reauthorized before continuing?
- **Learning:** Can each person explain their role and limits? What question or
  demonstration needs attention before the task proceeds?

## Closeout and Handoff

- **Disposition:** Is the equipment ready for authorized return to service, left
  isolated, awaiting parts, unsafe to operate, or being handed off?
  Running equipment is not the only acceptable outcome.
- **Return-to-service process:** If applicable, who follows the approved procedure
  for inspection, personnel clearance, device removal, notification, and restart?
  This discussion does not authorize removal of anyone else's lock.
- **Outstanding work:** What remains unresolved, what restrictions apply, and who
  owns the next action?
- **Official documentation:** Have findings, disposition, required notifications,
  and handoff details been recorded in the approved system of record?
- **Debrief:** What went well, what needs improvement, and what should the crew
  practice or learn next time?
