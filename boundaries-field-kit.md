# Diagnostic Boundary Protocols & Emotional Telemetry

This document outlines tactical communication frameworks and mental alignment protocols designed for high-risk, tight-space, and high-consequence environments (HVAC, mechanical bay, heavy rigging). 

When working in conditions that threaten life safety or well-being, external distractions and volatile internal states must be treated as unrefined data vectors to be isolated, sandboxed, and kept off the main operational track.

---

## 1. Internal Telemetry & Diagnostic Reminders
Use these mental checks when field conditions are severe, exhausting, or mentally compromising.

* **"Don't read the codes while the engine's still overheating."**
  * *Protocol:* Do not analyze your capability or career trajectory during an acute stress spike. Let the immediate chemical surge subside before running system diagnostics.
* **"That’s just feedback from a bad sensor; stick to the mechanical specs."**
  * *Protocol:* Isolate sudden flashes of deep shame or exhaustion as localized data loops caused by environment stress, not accurate telemetry of your reality.
* **"Log the pressure spike, but keep the relief valve open."**
  * *Protocol:* Acknowledge the presence of a severe system strain without letting the internal pressure crack the block or compromise tool handling.

---

## 2. Conversational Boundary Scripts
Clear, non-defensive responses designed to neutralize hazardous interpersonal behavior on the job site by tying the crossing of boundaries directly to an immediate stop-work consequence.

### A. Countering Projected Anger
* **Context:** A customer, supervisor, or coworker is blowing up or panicking over a job while you are in a hazardous or high-focus position.
* **Script:** *"Look, I get that you’re hot under the collar about this job, but I’m working on a heavy lift right here. If you need to blow off steam, take it out of this bay. If you keep yelling over my shoulder, I’m putting my tools down and walking away until you cool off. I value my skin too much to pull a wrench while you're creating a distraction."*

### B. Deflecting Guilt-Tripping
* **Context:** Pressure from others to cut corners on safety protocols (Lockout/Tagout, recovery tanks, line checks) to hit an unrealistic deadline.
* **Script:** *"I know everybody wants this rig rolling yesterday, but laying a guilt trip on me isn't going to make the grease move any faster. I'm checking the safety locks because I plan on walking out of here on my own two feet tonight. If you want to keep pushing me to cut corners, I’ll gladly hand you the wrench and you can crawl under this block yourself. Otherwise, let me do my diagnostics in peace."*

### C. Neutralizing Shame
* **Context:** Being mocked or heavily criticized for a slow diagnostic process or an honest misstep while trying to navigate an unsafe line layout.
* **Script:** *"We’ve all misread a diagnostic line or fought a rusted bolt before, so there's no need to make a production out of it. I’m tracking down the root cause so it gets fixed right the first time. If the commentary keeps up, I’m locking my box and letting you finish the teardown. Let me work, or take the job over."*

### D. Cutting Through Sarcasm
* **Context:** Passive-aggressive commentary while navigating uncomfortable, cramped, or high-heat workspaces.
* **Script:** *"Sarcasm isn’t going to get this manifold unbolted, and it sure as hell isn't helping me clear my head while I'm wedged in here. Let’s drop the smart talk. If you can’t give me a straight answer or a hand with this rig, I’m going to go work on the tractor in the back lot and leave you to figure this unit out on your own."*
