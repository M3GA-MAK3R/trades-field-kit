# Safety and Authorization Boundaries

These are educational discussion aids, not official paperwork, permits,
equipment-specific procedures, a hazard assessment, or authorization to work.
Use the [toolbox-talk aid](../templates/field-preflight.md) to reinforce preparation,
discipline, work ethic, and accountability. Keep actual job records private.
Stop when instructions, conditions, responsibilities, or qualifications are unclear.

## Hazardous Energy and Work Phases

Use the employer's applicable program and equipment-specific energy-control
procedure. Identify all sources, including separate control feeds, remote starts,
pressure, moving parts, heat, and stored electrical energy; control stored energy
and possible reaccumulation, not just the main disconnect
([OSHA 1910.147(d)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147)).

Electrical exposure in utilization installations is covered by Subpart S,
including [1910.333](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333);
1910.147 is not the sole rule for every electrical task
([1910.147(a)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147)).
These U.S. general-industry references do not establish which rules govern every
site; confirm jurisdiction, work classification, and employer requirements.

- **Isolated work:** The applicable procedure must establish and verify the safe
  condition before work on exposed parts. Treat unverified equipment as energized.
  A control switch, thermostat, interlock, or silent contactor is not proof of
  isolation; account for backfeed and induced voltage
  ([OSHA 1910.333(b)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333)).
- **Verification and instruments:** Electrical testing requires qualified persons,
  inspected instruments and accessories rated for the circuit and environment.
  Follow the approved instrument-verification method; do not substitute one generic
  CAT category, glove class, or displayed zero for the required assessment
  ([OSHA 1910.334(c)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.334)).
- **Operating readings:** Some observations require an operating system.
  Existing readings may be discussed, but this kit does not supply live-probing
  instructions. Stop and refer required energized electrical testing to a qualified,
  employer-authorized person under the applicable procedure
  ([OSHA 1910.333(a), (c)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333)).
- **Return to service:** Follow the applicable inspection, personnel-clearance,
  lock/tag-removal, notification, and restart requirements. Do not remove another
  person's device based on this kit; exceptional removal is governed by the
  employer's applicable procedure and regulatory conditions
  ([OSHA 1910.147(e)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147),
  [1910.333(b)(2)(v)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333)).
- **Continuity:** Group work, shift changes, and temporary testing require their
  applicable controls and restoration of protection before maintenance resumes.
  Left isolated, awaiting parts, or handed off can be valid outcomes
  ([OSHA 1910.147(f)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147)).

## Capacitors, Drives, and PPE

Stored electrical energy must be controlled before handling; capacitor-associated
equipment must be treated as energized during the required discharge work
([OSHA 1910.333(b)(2)(ii)(C)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333)).
Use the model-specific manufacturer procedure and approved, suitably rated tools,
including its discharge, grounding where applicable, waiting, and verification
requirements. This kit specifies no universal resistor value or waiting time and
does not direct improvised terminal shorting. A waiting period or an internal
bleeder alone is not a verification record.

Capacitance acceptance limits come from the component marking and manufacturer,
not a universal percentage. A continuity reading alone does not certify insulation
integrity or safe operation. Select PPE for the assessed task and hazards, including
shock, arc, chemical, and mechanical exposure; generic clothing or gloves do not
constitute a complete protective system
([OSHA 1910.335](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.335)).

## Coil Cleaning and Wet Work

Do not wash energized equipment or direct liquid into electrical compartments.
Isolation, covers, a GFCI, and drying each address different concerns; none
substitutes for the applicable energy-control and electrical-safety procedures.
Portable electrical equipment in conductive-liquid locations must be approved for
those locations, not merely used with extra PPE
([OSHA 1910.333(c)(9)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333),
[1910.334(a)(4)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.334)).

Read the exact coil/equipment instructions before selecting cleaner, pressure,
nozzle distance, direction, or fin tools. Do not assume a pressure washer is
permitted. For example, Johnson Controls TMAL bulletin TPM-SI0245 prohibits pressure
washers, chemicals, and hard-tool contact on the microchannel coils it covers;
that model-specific prohibition is not a universal cleaning recipe
([Johnson Controls service information](https://docs.johnsoncontrols.com/ductedsystems/api/khub/documents/TUg78wHItRmyi4kpDTod4w/content)).
If instructions are unavailable or incompatible with the proposed method, stop and
clarify. Follow the cleaner label, SDS, site drainage rules, and approved
return-to-service checks where applicable.

## Refrigerant Work and Interpretation

Discuss calculations using existing authorized readings. Attaching or detaching
gauges and adding/removing refrigerant can require the applicable Section 608
certification. Any apprentice exception must meet EPA's requirements; informal
supervision is not assumed sufficient
([EPA certification requirements](https://www.epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-requirements),
[EPA technician Q&A](https://www.epa.gov/section608/epas-refrigerant-management-program-questions-and-answers-section-608-certified)).

Use the identified refrigerant, correct pressure units, paired pressure/temperature
locations, operating mode, stable conditions, and equipment-specific target.
Pressure patterns or one superheat/subcooling value do not uniquely diagnose
charge or authorize an adjustment. R32, R454B, and other unlisted refrigerants
remain unsupported by these calculators; do not substitute another curve.
See [PT sources and limits](../references/sources.md).
