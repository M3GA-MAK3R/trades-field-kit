# Mechanic Shadow / Helper Learning Checklist

A first-year learning and discussion aid for use with an assigned mechanic,
mentor, or supervisor. Not official paperwork, a competency sign-off, task
authorization, or a replacement for discipline and work ethic.
Read [Safety and Authorization Boundaries](../SAFETY.md) first.
Keep completed learning notes and job records in approved private systems;
public contributions are blank templates and sanitized examples only.

## Before the Assignment

- [ ] Confirm the assigned mechanic, learning objective, work scope, and what you
  may observe or assist with. Ask what you must not touch or do.
- [ ] Review [arrival/contact/waiting arrangements](../../templates/field-preflight.md#arrival-contact-and-waiting),
  required check-in, escort, communication method, and approved waiting location.
- [ ] Ask which equipment instructions and safety procedures govern this task,
  and who holds the required qualifications and authorization.
- [ ] Agree where you should stand, how to stay clear of hazards, when questions
  are welcome, and how either person will call a stop.
- [ ] Identify permitted helper tasks, suitable tools/PPE, staging, and cleanup
  expectations. Do not start a task because it appears routine.

## Observe With Purpose

- [ ] Ask: What should the system be doing, what is actually happening, and which
  evidence supports the next step?
- [ ] Locate components conceptually using the correct manual/schematic; explain
  the sequence back without treating a generic diagram as wiring instructions.
- [ ] Ask how the mechanic separates symptom, possible cause, confirmed finding,
  and unresolved question. Avoid guessing a diagnosis to appear confident.
- [ ] Discuss existing authorized readings with units, conditions, locations, and
  the specification source. Do not obtain readings to complete this checklist.
- [ ] Notice preparation, tool care, communication, housekeeping, and handoff,
  not just the repair. Ask what would make the mechanic stop or change the plan.

## Assist Only Within the Agreed Scope

- [ ] Read back each assigned task, intended result, limits, and required check-back.
  Say when a term or instruction is unclear.
- [ ] Confirm supervision is available as required. If the mechanic leaves or the
  scope changes, pause rather than assuming permission to continue.
- [ ] Stage or pass tools/materials only as directed, keeping access and egress clear.
- [ ] Keep hands and tools clear of equipment outside your authorized task.
  This aid supplies no live electrical, stored-energy, or refrigerant-work procedure.
- [ ] Report discrepancies, damaged tools, missing parts, mistakes, and uncertainty
  promptly. Do not conceal an error, improvise, or bypass a safeguard.
- [ ] Report completion for the assigned person's review rather than calling the
  equipment safe or returning it to service yourself.

## Debrief With the Mechanic

- [ ] Explain one thing learned, the evidence behind it, and one remaining question.
- [ ] Ask: What did I do well? What should I change? What should I practice next?
- [ ] Agree one next learning opportunity, who will supervise/review it, and when
  to revisit it. Learning support does not change authorization.
- [ ] Clarify safe equipment disposition, outstanding work, tool accounting,
  cleanup, and the official handoff before leaving.

## Capture Progress for the First-Year Review

Use the [first-year review worksheet](../../templates/first-year-review.md) privately.
Separate **observed**, **practiced under supervision**, **demonstrated to an
assigned reviewer**, **not yet exposed**, and **needs refresher**. These are
learning descriptions, not certifications or permission to work independently.

For each example, record the skill, what you actually did, supervision/support
needed, feedback, next step, and a permitted private evidence reference. Record a
reviewer's acknowledgment only if they actually provide it; do not invent approval.
Create public portfolio examples only with permission and identifying details removed.
